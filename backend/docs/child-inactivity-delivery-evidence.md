# Inactivity warning delivery evidence — candidate

This is a disabled verification building block, not a configured email service, an activated warning, or permission to delete an account. The public release remains unchanged.

The owner's nonresponse decision allows deletion after the additional waiting period even if a warning goes unread or to Spam. It does not mean a failed or unsent warning should start that period. Google explicitly warns that a Gmail API HTTP 200 can occur even when a message is not successfully sent after a quota overrun. Gmail's `SENT` label can also be applied to an inserted message rather than an actual send. Neither a 200 response, a `SENT` label, nor the absence of a bounce is sufficient evidence on its own. See [Gmail error handling](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/guides/handle-errors) and [system labels](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/guides/labels).

An option to evaluate is a transactional mail provider with a signed recipient-server delivery event. For example, [Resend's delivery event](https://resend.com/docs/webhooks/emails/delivered) means the receiving server accepted a message; it does not establish inbox placement or that a parent read it. Delivery to an Apple private relay establishes only acceptance by the relay, not forwarding to the parent's actual inbox. A changed or disabled relay, bounce, complaint, suppression, lost event, conflicting records, or unknown recipient must prevent automatic action until resolved. No provider account has been created, no terms accepted, and no parent's information transmitted.

The candidate `inactivityDeliveryEvent.ts` checks a raw signed Svix event against an already-persisted provider message ID, exact sender and single recipient, the attempt's creation time, and a trusted current time. It distinguishes a direct address from an Apple relay and returns only a minimal observation. Signed bounce, failure, suppression, delay and complaint events become **holds**, not delivery; a future consumer must persist and reconcile them even when they arrive after or out of order with a delivery event. It validates the raw bytes and rejects malformed signatures, unexpected event types, timestamps, or bindings. A verified observation is **not** a durable receipt and does not start the 12-month clock or authorize deletion. The caller must use a raw request body and a separately protected signing secret; see [Resend webhook verification](https://resend.com/docs/webhooks/verify-webhooks-requests) and [Svix's verification specification](https://docs.svix.com/receiving/verifying-payloads/how-manual).

Before this can be used: qualify the provider and its processing terms; configure the sender domain and Apple relay; authenticate and authorize the owner; bind each outbound send to a durable unique attempt and the exact notice/current parent contact; protect the webhook secret; implement authenticated, replay-safe independent persistence and event deduplication; process later failure and contact changes; integrate the SQL and restore guards; run synthetic delivery, duplicate, bounce, relay and recovery rehearsals; and update the bilingual notice and store disclosures. An uncertain send or missing event holds; do not blindly resend or backdate a timer. The production inactivity deployment guard stays in place.

As of 4 November 2026, [Resend's published free plan](https://resend.com/pricing) lists 3,000 emails/month and 100/day, and its [security page](https://resend.com/security) says email and log data are retained for 30 days. Limits, availability and terms can change, and exceeding free limits or other cloud usage may cost money. The extra year's evidence therefore cannot depend on the provider keeping its own log. Any use of a new processor, its charges, and actual data-retention/deletion behavior must be reviewed before activation.
