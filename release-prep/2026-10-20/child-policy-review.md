## United States: COPPA

**Current timing.** The amended rule was published **22 April 2025**, effective **23 June 2025**; the ordinary operator compliance deadline was **22 April 2026**. The separate exceptions to that deadline concern Safe Harbor program provisions. The new requirements are already relevant to this launch. [Federal Register final rule, DATES](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-04-22/html/2025-05904.htm).

**Audience and collection.** COPPA's mixed-audience category concerns a child-directed service whose primary audience is not children. It is not established just by choosing “all ages.” Record the intended audience, content and marketing rationale. The FTC permits age differentiation before covered collection; known under-13 users need consent or a valid exception. Guest delivery/security identifiers still need a documented purpose and exception assessment. [FTC six-step plan, Steps 1–3](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule-six-step-compliance-plan-your-business).

**Manual forms have a recognized basis.** Current §312.5(b)(2)(i) expressly includes a parent-signed form returned by electronic scan, mail or fax. The overarching test remains reasonable efforts to ensure the consenting person is the parent. Google/Apple account control or an adult checkbox alone is not that conclusion. Separate verifiable permission is required for non-integral third-party disclosure; optional public scores should remain separately declined without losing the private account. A mere reply email is a different, limited method. [16 CFR §312.5(a)–(c)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-312/section-312.5).

**Age-screen recommendation — source follow-up completed.** FTC staff FAQ D.7 recommends neutral entry and measures against changing age through back-buttoning. The former retry gap is now addressed by the protective native merge, tab marker and signed anonymous cookie; their scope and limits are in the age-guard receipt. Real behavior remains to be qualified. This is not an FTC approval and does not prescribe exact birth-date collection. [FTC FAQ, D.5–D.7](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions).

**Operational requirements beyond the form.** Maintain a written security program with a named coordinator, risk assessments and program review at least annually, safeguard testing, and written security assurances from relevant providers. The owner's role can include coordinating this work; naming an operator does not replace it. [16 CFR §312.8(b)–(c)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-312/section-312.8). Maintain and publish a retention policy with each purpose, retention need and deletion timeframe. The current notice's unspecified backup/security schedules and undefined “active account” lifetime need concrete operational definitions; the stated 14-day form deletion periods also need an executable process. [16 CFR §312.10](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-312/section-312.10).

## Brazil: consent and age assurance are separate

**Applicable scope.** Law **15.211/2025**, effective **17 March 2026**, covers products directed to children/adolescents **or likely accessed by them**. Articles 12(III) and 14 require measures to receive store/OS age information; this is broader than social networks or loot boxes. Article 9's reliable verification at each access and prohibition on self-declaration concern inappropriate/prohibited under-18 content; do not turn that narrower provision into a claim that every family game must collect identity documents. [ECA Digital, arts. 1, 9–15](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2023-2026/2025/lei/l15211.htm).

Decree **12.880, 18 March 2026**, arts. 24–27, requires proportionate, reliable, minimal and auditable mechanisms. Article 25 §4 requires the more protective conflicting signal, and users need an age-contestation route. Article 26's browser provision sits within its inappropriate-content obligation; it must not be described as universal identity verification for every web game. Assess the actual content and website assurance separately. [Decree, arts. 24–27](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2023-2026/2026/decreto/d12880.htm).

**ANPD status refreshed 21 October.** The portal still labels age-assurance guidance preliminary/under consolidation. Separately, Circuit 30 closed **16 October 2026** and approved the general scope/obligations guide with changes and subsequent publication directed; a final consolidated publication was not located. The decision package links the minutes and approved vote. A timetable is not publication evidence or postponement of the law. [ANPD portal](https://www.gov.br/anpd/pt-br/assuntos/eca-digital/), [Circuit 30 minutes](https://www.gov.br/anpd/pt-br/assuntos/deliberacoes-do-conselho-diretor/circuitos-deliberativos-ano-2026/cd-30-2026-ata.pdf/@@display-file/file).

The March guidance, **pp. 7–13**, recommends assessing both service risks and verification risks, documenting accuracy/robustness, and warns that self-declaration alone is weak evidence. It favors proportionate, privacy-preserving alternatives rather than indiscriminate document/biometric collection. A short Ludolume-specific risk/assurance decision record is needed. [ANPD preliminary guidance, March 2026](https://www.gov.br/anpd/pt-br/assuntos/eca-digital/mecanismos-confiaveis-de-afericao-de-idade-orientacoes-preliminares.pdf/@@download/file).

**Consent boundary.** LGPD art. 14 includes best interests, specific highlighted parental consent for children, reasonable efforts to verify the responsible person, minimal collection and understandable notices. Prepare understandable BR parent/child instructions; pt-BR is the practical recommendation for this market. [LGPD art. 14 §§1–6](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2015-2018/2018/lei/l13709compilado.htm). ANPD's **Enunciado 1, 22 May 2023**, permits other statutory bases where best interests prevail. Consequently, the candidate's BR parent-required-below-18 rule is a protective product policy, not a finding that all Brazilian under-18 processing universally requires the same consent mechanism. Keep the chosen basis and purpose explicit. [ANPD Enunciado 1](https://bibliotecadigital.mj.gov.br/bitstream/1/10215/2/Enunciado_ANPD_2023_1.html).

## Store declarations and SDKs

**Apple:** Guidelines **1.3, 5.1.4 and 4.8** distinguish Kids Category requirements, children's privacy, and equivalent privacy-preserving login. Including children does not itself require selecting Kids Category, but metadata must not imply children are the primary audience outside that category. A parental gate is distinct from data consent. If Kids Category is selected, external links and third-party data behavior need its additional review. Keep adult Google/Apple login behind eligibility and child nickname/password login available. [Current App Review Guidelines, checked 2026-10-20](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/).

**Google:** Selecting any child audience triggers Families requirements. Declare actual age groups, Data safety and IARC content truthfully; authentication data is included. Non-child-approved SDKs/APIs in a mixed-audience app must be behind a neutral age screen or avoid child collection, and cannot be mandatory for access to content. Advertising identifiers must not be transmitted for children or unknown-age users. Ad SDK certification is conditional on serving ads; a free download alone does not establish an ad-free app. [Families policy, requirements 3–5](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335?hl=en). The neutral-screen guidance rejects an older-age preset or prompts that encourage falsification. [Target audience / Neutral age screen](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9867159).
